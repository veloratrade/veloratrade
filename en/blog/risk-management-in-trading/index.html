Risk Management in Trading: How to Control Losses and Protect Capital

Risk management is the most important skill for every trader. Without it, even the best strategy can destroy your account.

Trading journalRisk managementPerformance analytics
8 min read VELORA

Risk per trade

Many traders define a fixed percentage of capital to risk on each trade. The exact number depends on the trader, but consistency matters. A journal reveals when you increase risk emotionally or abandon your plan after wins or losses.

Risk-to-reward and R-multiple

A trade should be evaluated not only by profit or loss, but by how much was risked to achieve that result. R-multiple helps normalize performance across different markets and position sizes.

Drawdown rules

A strong plan defines when to stop trading. Daily drawdown limits, weekly limits, and maximum account drawdown rules help protect capital. Journaling shows whether you respected those limits.

Finding high-risk patterns

Your journal may show that certain symbols, sessions, or setups create most of your drawdown. This allows you to reduce exposure, adjust position sizing, or remove weak conditions from your playbook.

Final thoughts

Risk management is not just a calculation. It is repeated behavior. A journal helps you measure whether your risk behavior matches your trading plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a common risk per trade?

Many traders use 0.5% to 2%, but the right amount depends on strategy, account size, and risk tolerance.

Is risk-to-reward always more important than win rate?

They must be reviewed together. A low win rate can work with high R, and a high win rate can fail with poor R.

How does journaling improve risk management?

It reveals where you broke risk rules and which conditions produced the largest losses.
